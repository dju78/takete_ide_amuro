# Historical Claims Review & Editorial Guidelines

This document provides a systematic editorial review and categorization of historical claims found in the supplied Takete-Ide community historical account (`takete-history-original.md`). It establishes boundaries for what can be presented on the public website, what requires contextual labelling, and what must remain subject to further research and community verification.

---

## Editorial Principles & Attribution Standard

When writing content for the public website:
- **Attribution First**: Always frame non-archival traditions with precise attribution phrases:
  - *“According to community historical accounts…”*
  - *“Oral tradition records…”*
  - *“Community tradition associates…”*
  - *“The supplied historical account describes…”*
- **No Inferred Science or Supernatural Certainty**: Spiritual, oracle, or metaphysical narratives (e.g. divination verdicts, miraculous protections) must be presented strictly as cultural beliefs, sacred memories, and oral traditions—never as independently verified scientific facts.
- **Centenary Precision**: Clearly distinguish between the ancient origins of Takete-Ide across earlier settlements and the relocation to the present low-plain site around 1926.

## 1. Suitable for Cautious Public Use

These claims are broad geographic, administrative, linguistic, and established modern community facts that can be referenced publicly with standard editorial framing:

1. **Administrative & Regional Context**:
   - Takete-Ide is one of the seven historic towns/villages comprising the Amuro District in MopAmuro LGA, Kogi State, Nigeria.
   - Amuro District comprises Takete-Ide, Orokere, Aiyede, Okagi, Aiyedayo/Iloke, Otafun, and Aiyeteju Agbajogun.
   - Takete-Ide is located approximately 18 km from Mopa (LGA headquarters) and 16 km off the Kabba–Ilorin road at Effo-Amuro.
   - An Area Office of MopAmuro LGA has been situated in Takete-Ide since the creation of the LGA in 1991.

2. **Linguistic & Cultural Identity**:
   - Takete-Ide people are part of the Iyagba/Yagba linguistic and cultural branch of Okun Yoruba people in the Confluence region.
   - The greeting *“Okun”* expresses vitality, strength, and goodwill across Okun sub-groups (Iyagba, Owe, Bunu, Ijumu, Gbede, Kiri, Oworo).

3. **Geographic Topography & Water Bodies**:
   - Takete-Ide sits on a fertile plain surrounded by hills, including Oke Elegan, Obasoro Hill, and Oroke Agodi.
   - Water bodies such as Eba River, Owowo River, Oga, and local streams (Ibedo, Igan, Gboruku, Pandara, Omi Loke) form historical landmarks.

4. **Etymology & Suffix**:
   - The name *Takete-Idera* (frequently shortened to *Takete-Ide*) carries the community meaning of being separated or established in a place of comfort and peace.

## 2. Community Historical Account

These accounts form the backbone of the community's collective memory regarding migration, regional conflict, and resettlement. They should be published with appropriate attribution (*“According to community historical accounts…”*):

1. **Amuro Dispersal from Amuro-Odo**:
   - The narrative that Amuro towns originally shared a settlement at Amuro-Odo before being dispersed by 19th-century regional warfare and Nupe raids.
2. **Sequential Migration Stages**:
   - Movement from Amuro-Odo to *Igbo Ide* (Igbo Idera), a forested refuge east of the original settlement.
   - Subsequent dispersal following renewed raids, leading southwards to *Ilu-Oke* (the town on the hill/uplands).
   - Temporary relocation of segments of the community further into the hills at *Okeata* during heightened conflicts.
3. **Descent to the Present Settlement (c.1926)**:
   - The collective movement of community quarters (Odo Takete, Oke Adan, Oke Ako) from the upland settlement of Ilu-Oke down to the fertile plain (initially called *Ileteju*, later affirmed as *Takete-Idera*).
   - Occurred *in 1926 or thereabouts*.
4. **Shared Brotherhood with Neighboring Communities**:
   - Close kinship and shared migration history between Takete-Ide and neighboring Otafun (originating together at Igbo Ide).

## 3. Oral Tradition

Oral traditions represent spoken lore passed down through generations. They should be explicitly framed as oral traditions (*“Oral tradition records…”* / *“Legend recounts…”*):

1. **Yagba Ancestral Origin Stories**:
   - *Tradition 1 (Ile-Ife)*: A prince from Ile-Ife lost part of his conquest territory and lamented *“Iya agba lo jemi”* (lack of an elder to advise him), leading to the nickname Iyagba. (Prevalent in Yagba East and MopAmuro).
   - *Tradition 2 (Old-Oyo)*: Descendants of a princess/elderly woman (*Iya-agba*) who migrated from Oyo-Ile seeking peace. (Prevalent in Yagba West).
2. **Otafun Separation Narratives**:
   - *Account 1*: A white cloth textile specialist led the group that chose to settle near white cloth trade/weaving (*Otafunfun*).
   - *Account 2*: A prominent white stone attracted the group (*Okuta funfun*), from which Otafun took its name.
3. **Pa Thomas Ode & the Hunter's Inquest**:
   - The tradition identifying hunter Pa Thomas Ode as the forerunner who surveyed the low-plain forest and reported its fertility and safety to his colleagues and community elders at Ilu-Oke.

## 4. Cultural / Spiritual Tradition

Sacred traditions, symbols, and spiritual protections must be respected and presented as living cultural heritage and community memory, never converted into empirical/physical claims:

1. **Agbagba Ide as Community Sanctuary & Symbol**:
   - The sacred cave/natural refuge of *Agbagba Ide* revered as divine deliverance (*Olorun/Olodumare*) and spiritual protection during 19th-century warfare.
   - The creation of portable symbols/prototypes of Agbagba Ide taken as protective emblems.
   - The *Oriki Agbagba Ide* and the *Takete-Ide Community Anthem* celebrating Agbagba Ide.
2. **Traditional Divination & Spiritual Fortification**:
   - Traditions regarding *Obi Ifa* consultation and hunters' clairvoyance in selecting settlement grounds.
3. **Amuro Pot of Arrows (*Amu Oro*)**:
   - The symbolic pot containing seven poisonous arrows as a legendary defensive deterrent.

## 5. Requires Verification

These specific claims from the manuscript contain factual assertions, measurements, or references that need documentary cross-referencing and verification before being treated as authoritative:

1. **Geographic Coordinates**:
   - Latitude 8.16667, Longitude 6.03333 cited in text — verify with standard geospatial surveys and GPS mapping of Takete-Ide.
2. **Archaeological & Metallurgical Age**:
   - Statements asserting *“remarkable evidence of iron-works and brass casting lending credence to its ancient age of three centuries”* — requires formal archaeological documentation or reference to published studies (e.g. Obayemi or regional metallurgical surveys).
3. **Alamuro Stool Timeline**:
   - The specific note that the Alamuro title was *“created only in 1934”* and rotation rules — verify against British colonial gazettes, administrative files, and official Chieftaincy Declarations.
4. **First Born at Present Site**:
   - Identification of Pa George Fiki as the first child born at the new settlement site (c.1926) — cross-check with family and community records.

## 6. Do Not Publish as Settled Fact Yet

The following items have incomplete data, conflicting interpretations, or formatting anomalies that must **NOT** be published as verified facts on public pages until resolved:

1. **Olu'de Chronological List & Family Alignments**:
   - The 12 predecessor Olu'des and 13th Olu'de (Oba Philip Ebilakun) listed at the end of the manuscript suffer from corrupted column alignments in the source copy (family and ward names separated from rulers).
   - Incomplete reign dates (e.g. *“Oba Joshua Fiki reigned from 198× - 200×”*) must not be guessed or published as full dates.
2. **Precise Universal Calendar Dates for Pre-1926 Eras**:
   - Exact calendar years for the founding of Amuro-Odo, stay at Igbo Ide, or movement to Ilu-Oke are unrecorded; only the relative 19th-century war era is noted.
3. **"Takete-Ide Founded in 1926" (Forbidden Error)**:
   - It is strictly forbidden to claim that Takete-Ide was founded in 1926. The 1926 date marks the descent from Ilu-Oke to the current plain, not the inception of the community.
