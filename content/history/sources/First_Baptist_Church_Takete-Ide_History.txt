COMMUNITY HERITAGE
A Brief History of
First Baptist Church, Takete-Ide
The birth and growth of the Baptist Mission in Takete-Ide
EDITORIAL NOTE  This is a carefully edited transcription of the historical account provided in two photographed 
pages. Grammar and punctuation have been corrected for readability. Names, dates and historical claims should 
be confirmed with church elders before the text is published on the Takete-Ide website.
The Birth of the Baptist Mission
The original account presents baptism as the mark of complete acceptance of the gospel of Jesus Christ. 
It explains that baptism signified that a believer had abandoned past deeds regarded as sinful and had 
accepted the teachings of the Holy Bible. One important principle emphasised by the early missionaries 
was the doctrine of one man and one wife.
Some of the men who responded to the Christian faith were polygamous. The missionaries would not 
agree to baptise them unless they separated from one of their wives. Some made this courageous but 
difficult decision, while others were unwilling to do so.
In 1922, Rev. Tommy Titcombe visited Takete-Ide while preparations were being made for the first 
baptism in the community. He reiterated the Sudan Interior Mission (SIM) doctrine of one man and one 
wife. This requirement did not sit well with some new converts, who did not want to sacrifice any of 
their wives as a condition of the new faith and baptism. Having accepted Christianity and not wishing to 
return to their former way of life, some chose a different Christian denomination. At that time, the 
Baptist Mission was already firmly established in Mopa. The group, led by Pa Joash Agunbiade, therefore 
embraced the Baptist Mission.

The Founding of First Baptist Church
In November 1922, six men, with the cooperation of their wives, established the First Baptist Church in 
Takete-Ide. The founders listed in the original account were:
1. Pa Joash Agunbiade
2. Pa Luke Olorunleke
3. Pa Paul Amora
4. Pa Saul Akedi (Baba Eleahy)
5. Pa Noah Omoyele
6. Pa Alfred Eseyin (Baba Owa)
Pa Joash Agunbiade served as Baba Egbe, Pa Luke Olorunleke was the Church Leader, and the source 
identifies Noah Eseyin as the Church Secretary.
Early Worship Places and Church Buildings
At the Church's first location, members of the SIM (ECWA) congregation asked the Baptist worshippers 
to leave because the two places of worship were too close and the services were disturbing one 
another. The Baptist congregation relocated to another place. Its building was made of mud, and mud 
seats were also constructed because there was no money to buy chairs.
The Church later moved to its present location, where a small auditorium with a thatched roof was built. 
In 1959, the thatch was replaced with corrugated iron sheets. A new auditorium was built in the middle 
of the 1980s.
Early Pastoral Support
Around 1924, Rev. Margi, an Igede Baptist minister, visited the young Church regularly. He would stay 
for one or two weeks at a time and slept inside the small auditorium.
In 1925, the Church received its first pastor, Rev. Agbode, who also came from Igede. He was followed 
by Pastor Olaleken, Pastor David Otitodun and Pastor Adeosun. Primary-school teachers posted to the 
community also served as pastors. They included Pastor Ajibade from Oyo State and Pastor Falana from 
Ekirin-Adde. A student pastor, Pastor Adetunji from Saki in Oyo State, also served in the Church for three 
months.

Pastoral Leadership, 1982-1990
HRH Oba Philip Ebilakun, the reigning Olude of Takete-Ide at the time of the source account, served as 
Church Pastor from 1982 to 1985. Rev. Oyekunle served from 1985 until 1988. Oba Ebilakun then 
stepped in again as pastor from 1988 to 1990.
Rev. Agbogun was inducted as Church Pastor in August 1990 and was ordained a full minister on 14 
November 1990. The source states that he continued serving up to the date on which the original 
account was written.
The Aiyedayo Road and Church Land
As the community developed and expanded, a new access road to Oroke (Aiyedayo) was needed from 
the Otafun/Aghara axis and the Takete-Ide/Aiyede axis. The most direct route from Oroke would have 
met the road opposite the primary school's main gate. Community leaders considered a junction directly 
in front of the school too risky. They therefore approached the Church for permission to route the road 
through its land, farther away from the school.
The Church agreed, and its land was partitioned when the Aiyedayo road was constructed through the 
property. One portion fell on the side of Pa Agunbiade's land, while another remained on the side of the 
Church's present location. From the beginning, the Church shared boundaries with Pa Agunbiade on the 
Oke Adan side and with Pa Elehire on the Olorunsogo side. The land extended towards the Oke-Ako 
bypass near the house of Baba Ajayi Yeloyejo.
Growth and the Legacy of Pa Alfred Eseyin
From its small auditorium, the Church recorded notable achievements, particularly in expansion and 
growth. Pa Alfred Eseyin, known as Baba Owa, was the last surviving member of the six founders 
mentioned in the account. The writer's generation met him and benefited greatly from his teaching and 
example.
He attended the early-morning prayer service at 5:00 a.m., the evening service and regular Sunday 
services. He also taught in the Sunday School. He was often invited to say the closing prayer and was 
remembered for concluding it in a distinctive way:
“Bope wani, bo yawa ni, odo Re na lanmbo wa simi. Kiwa kabo ka le je omo odo rere lodo Re, 
ni oruko ti Jesu Kristi Oluwa wa. Amin.”

The Church Farm and Later Leadership
From the beginning, the Church maintained a farm to generate income. Farm produce was sold to 
support the smooth running of the Church. Fridays were usually set aside for members, both male and 
female, to work together on the Church farm.
As the founders grew old and some passed away, their children and other members assumed greater 
responsibility. As the Church expanded to include almost the entire community, the following people 
were remembered as having played leading roles in its affairs:
• Pa Raphael Elewa
• Pa Thomas Eleah
• Pa Gideon Eleah
• Pa Timothy Adetimoyo
• Pa David Olorunleke
• Pa Akin Eseyin
• Chief Reuben Eleah
• Chief Matthew Igunrin Ayedogbon
• Pa Mark Agunbiade
The original account describes all of them as being of blessed memory.
Points to Verify Before Website Publication
The photographs are clear enough for a reliable narrative transcription, but the following details should 
be checked against the Church register or confirmed by elders:
• The founders' list names Pa Noah Omoyele, while the next paragraph identifies Noah Eseyin as 
Church Secretary.
• Confirm the spellings of Baba Eleahy, Pastor Olaleken, Pa Timothy Adetimoyo and Chief Matthew 
Igunrin Ayedogbon.
• Confirm the spelling of Baba Ajayi Yeloyejo and the boundary name Pa Elehire.
• Record the date on which the original history was written, especially because it says Rev. Agbogun 
had served ‘till date’.
Source: Two-page photographed church history supplied for the Takete-Ide community website.
